The Future of Learning: Speaking Cards

Based on Luis von Ahn's TED Talk on Duolingo and the future of education
CARD 1

Rethinking What "Education" Even Means

From the talk: Von Ahn says traditional education tries to cover too much at once. Duolingo's founders decided to focus on just one subject — language learning — because it reaches a vast global audience and can directly raise people's income.
Discuss
  • If you had to redesign school from scratch and could only teach one subject really well, which subject would you choose, and why?
  • Do you think language learning deserves more attention in schools than it usually gets? Explain your reasoning.
  • What's one skill you wish you had learned earlier in life, and how would learning it have changed things for you?
Useful language
vastExtremely large in size or amount. to broadenTo make something wider or more general. realmAn area of activity, interest, or knowledge.
CARD 2

Who Actually Gets Access to Education?

From the talk: Building schools everywhere is far too expensive, but most of the world already owns a smartphone. Duolingo uses a freemium model: it's free for everyone, but well-off users in richer countries pay to remove ads, which quietly funds free access for learners in poorer countries.
Discuss
  • Von Ahn calls this a form of "wealth redistribution." Do you think it's fair to expect wealthier users to fund free access for others? Why or why not?
  • Besides smartphones, what other tools or systems could realistically bring quality education to people with very little money?
  • Is access to a smartphone really the same as access to a good education? What's still missing?
Useful language
well-offHaving a comfortable amount of money; financially secure. wealthA large amount of money or valuable possessions. insecurityA lack of confidence or a lack of safety and stability.
CARD 3

Broccoli vs. Dessert: Competing with Social Media

From the talk: Von Ahn compares delivering education through a smartphone to serving broccoli right next to the most delicious dessert imaginable. Apps like TikTok and Instagram are built to be addictive, so an educational app has to compete for the exact same attention.
Discuss
  • Do you think it's actually possible for a learning app to feel as exciting as social media? Why does it barely ever happen?
  • Think about your own phone habits. Which app has the strongest pull on your attention, and what exactly makes it so hard to put down?
  • Should schools compete with social media for students' attention, or should they take a completely different approach instead?
Useful language
barelyAlmost not; only just. craveTo want something very strongly. to hoardTo collect and store large amounts of something.
CARD 4

Streaks, Notifications, and the Psychology of Habit

From the talk: A visible daily streak, plus notifications timed around a person's usual free time, keep millions of users coming back. Von Ahn even admits Duolingo's notifications can be "passive-aggressive" — but he argues it works, and that it helps people actually reach a goal instead of quietly giving up.
Discuss
  • Where's the line between a helpful reminder and manipulative design? Give a real example from an app you use.
  • Have you ever kept doing something — a habit, a class, a hobby — mainly because you didn't want to "lose your streak"? What happened?
  • If you were designing a habit-building feature for a study app, what would you add, and what would you deliberately leave out?
Useful language
to reach a goalTo succeed in achieving something you were aiming for. opt outTo choose not to take part in something. mayhemWild confusion and disorder.
CARD 5

A Future Where Screen Time Isn't the Enemy

From the talk: Von Ahn's hope is a future where screen time isn't automatically seen as a bad thing, because a phone can deliver real, high-quality education to anyone, rich or poor. He believes the same tricks that keep people scrolling could eventually help people learn math, physics, or almost anything else.
Discuss
  • Do you share this hope, or do you think screen time will always come with a hidden cost, no matter the content? Explain.
  • Ten years from now, how do you think people your age will be learning new skills? Will classrooms still exist?
  • What's one fear you have about the future of technology and learning — and, on the other hand, one thing that gives you hope?

fearA strong feeling caused by danger, worry, or threat. thereforeAs a result; for this reason. to sink inTo be fully understood or realized, often after some time.
